Basic series RLC circuit

The resistor, inductor and capacitor are connected one after another in a single loop with an AC source, so the same current flows through all three.

Definitions of each term

Voltage V, volt
The electrical push from the source that drives current around the circuit. Here it is the RMS value of an AC supply.
Current I, ampere
The flow of electric charge through the circuit. In a series circuit it is the same in R, L and C.
Frequency f, hertz
How many complete AC cycles occur each second. Mains supply is 50 Hz in India.
Resistance R, ohm (Ω)
Opposition to current that turns electrical energy into heat. It does not depend on frequency.
Inductance L, henry (H)
The property of a coil that opposes changes in current by storing energy in a magnetic field.
Capacitance C, farad (F)
The ability to store energy in an electric field between two plates, opposing changes in voltage.
Inductive reactance XL, Ω
AC opposition from an inductor. It increases with frequency: XL = 2πfL.
Capacitive reactance XC, Ω
AC opposition from a capacitor. It decreases with frequency: XC = 1 ÷ 2πfC.
Net reactance X, Ω
The difference XL − XC. Positive is inductive, negative is capacitive, zero is resonance.
Impedance Z, Ω
The total opposition to AC from R and X combined: Z = √(R² + X²).
Phase angle φ, degree
The angle by which the current is ahead of or behind the source voltage.
Voltage drops VR, VL, VC
The voltage across each component, found as the current times its R, XL or XC.
Active power P, watt
The real power used up as heat in the resistor: P = I²R.
Reactive power Q, var
Power that moves back and forth between the source and the L and C without being used up.
Apparent power S, VA
The total power the source supplies, S = V × I, made up of P and Q.
Power factor PF
The fraction of apparent power that is real power: PF = R ÷ Z = cos φ.
Resonance f₀, hertz
The frequency where XL = XC, so Z is lowest and the current is highest.
Quality factor Q
How sharp the resonance is. A higher Q means a narrower, taller resonance peak.

Explanation

A series RLC circuit connects a resistor, an inductor and a capacitor one after another across an AC source, so the same current flows through all three. This page explains what each result means and how it is found.

1. Reactance

Inductors and capacitors resist AC without dissipating energy. This opposition is called reactance and is measured in ohms.

Inductive: XL = 2πfL. It grows with frequency.

Capacitive: XC = 1 ÷ 2πfC. It shrinks with frequency.

The net reactance is X = XL − XC. Positive means inductive, negative means capacitive.

2. Impedance and phase

Resistance and net reactance are at 90° to each other, so they add like the sides of a right triangle.

Z = √(R² + X²)

The phase angle φ = tan⁻¹(X ÷ R) tells how far the current is behind or ahead of the source voltage. Positive φ means the current lags.

3. Current and voltage drops

Ohm's law for AC gives the current: I = V ÷ Z.

Each part then drops its own voltage: VR = IR, VL = IXL, VC = IXC.

VR is in phase with I, VL leads by 90° and VC lags by 90°. Because of this, VL and VC can each be larger than the source voltage near resonance.

4. Power

Active power P = I²R (watts) is the real power turned into heat in the resistor.

Reactive power Q = I²X (var) swings back and forth between the source and the L and C.

Apparent power S = V·I (VA) combines both, and S² = P² + Q².

Power factor PF = R ÷ Z = cos φ shows how much of S is real power. It is 1 at resonance.

5. Resonance

At one special frequency XL equals XC, so they cancel and only R is left.

f₀ = 1 ÷ (2π√(LC))

There Z is at its minimum (equal to R), the current is at its maximum and the circuit behaves as purely resistive. Use the “Set to resonance” button in the calculator to try it.

6. Quality factor

Q = (1 ÷ R)√(L ÷ C)

Q measures how sharp the resonance is. A high Q (small R) gives a narrow, tall resonance peak. A low Q gives a broad, flat one. It also equals the ratio of VL or VC to the source voltage at resonance.
